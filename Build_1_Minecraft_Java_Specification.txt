BUILD #1 — MINECRAFT SURVIVAL COTTAGE
MASTER CONSTRUCTION SPECIFICATION
Minecraft Java Edition 1.20 / 1.21+ | Creative + Survival

PURPOSE
This document defines the construction, placement, collision, furniture, and testing standards for Build #1. It is designed for both Creative construction and Survival functionality.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION
The exact exterior footprint and room coordinates have not yet been selected. Therefore, this document defines a consistent coordinate system, exact block-state examples, and placement rules, but does not pretend that the sample coordinates form a complete house. A final block-by-block plan requires the chosen exterior width and depth.

============================================================
1. GLOBAL COORDINATE SYSTEM
============================================================

Use one coordinate convention throughout the build:

+X = East
-X = West
+Z = South
-Z = North
+Y = Up

Choose the front-left exterior foundation corner as the origin O = (0, 0, 0).

VERTICAL REFERENCE
- Foundation/floor block occupies Y = 0.
- Walking surface is at elevation Y = 1.
- Standing player's feet are at elevation Y = 1.
- Door lower half occupies Y = 1.
- Door upper half occupies Y = 2.
- A solid lintel may begin at Y = 3.

Do not confuse a block's integer Y coordinate with the top surface of that block. A full block at Y=0 has its top surface at elevation Y=1.

All coordinates in the final construction plan must be absolute integer coordinates or clearly defined relative coordinates. Minecraft commands do not calculate expressions such as X+1 or Z+1. Replace these expressions with actual numbers before running commands.

============================================================
2. PLAYER DIMENSIONS AND CLEARANCE
============================================================

Normal standing player dimensions:
- Width: 0.6 blocks.
- Depth: 0.6 blocks.
- Height: 1.8 blocks.
- Eye height: 1.62 blocks.

These dimensions apply to a normally standing player. Swimming, crawling, crouching, and sleeping use different poses.

GENERAL STANDING CLEARANCE
- Provide at least 2.0 blocks of unobstructed vertical space above the actual walking surface along normal walking routes.
- The 2.0-block clearance is a design allowance, not a universal Minecraft requirement for every pose or mechanic.
- Check the actual collision shapes of nearby blocks.
- Do not infer collision safety from visual appearance alone.

============================================================
3. DOORWAY AND THRESHOLD
============================================================

Example oak door states:

/setblock X 1 Z minecraft:oak_door[facing=south,half=lower,hinge=left,open=false,powered=false]
/setblock X 2 Z minecraft:oak_door[facing=south,half=upper,hinge=left,open=false,powered=false]

Replace X and Z with actual coordinates.

Both door halves must match in facing, hinge, open, and powered state. Only the half property differs.

DOORWAY REQUIREMENTS
1. Exterior porch, doorway, and interior floor must have the same intended walking elevation.
2. Do not place a bottom slab or raised block in the threshold if a flush transition is required.
3. Keep the doorway free of obstructing signs, fences, slabs, decorations, and other collision shapes.
4. Provide at least 2 blocks of standing clearance above the walking surface.
5. A solid lintel may begin at elevation Y=3 in this coordinate convention.
6. Check the collision shapes of stairs, slabs, and decorative arch blocks. A top-half stair or slab is not automatically safe.
7. Test the door in both open and closed states.
8. Ensure the open door does not obstruct the intended route.

A two-block-high doorway is sufficient for a normally standing player. A 2.5- or 3-block-high arch is an optional architectural choice, not a requirement.

============================================================
4. STAIRCASE AND STAIRWELL
============================================================

Java stair block states include:
- facing=north|south|east|west
- half=bottom|top
- shape=straight|inner_left|inner_right|outer_left|outer_right
- waterlogged=true|false

Example:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:oak_stairs[facing=east,half=bottom,shape=straight,waterlogged=false]

Use bottom-half stairs for a conventional staircase unless the design specifically calls for upside-down stairs.

STAIRCASE REQUIREMENTS
1. Maintain at least 2 blocks of vertical clearance above the actual walking surface throughout the intended route.
2. Measure from the stair's actual collision/walking surface, not simply from its block coordinate.
3. Check the entire player hitbox through each step transition.
4. Check both flights of the L-shaped staircase and the turning landing.
5. Remove ceiling and upper-floor blocks that intersect the stairwell clearance envelope.
6. Do not run horizontal beams through the player's clearance envelope.
7. Ensure the top landing connects directly to the loft floor.
8. Keep decorative trim outside the usable walking and headroom envelope.
9. Check roof slopes and rafters above the upper flight and landing.

A 2 x 4 stairwell opening is only a starting proposal. It is not a universal requirement. The final opening must be sized for the actual stair count, orientation, turning landing, player clearance, and upper-floor geometry.

Do not use a universal head-position formula such as Y_stair + 2.3. Stair collision and player foot position depend on the stair's shape and the player's position.

============================================================
5. FURNITURE AND FUNCTIONAL BLOCKS
============================================================

A. BED

Example states for a south-facing white bed:

/setblock X 1 Z minecraft:white_bed[facing=south,part=foot,occupied=false]
/setblock X 1 Z1 minecraft:white_bed[facing=south,part=head,occupied=false]

Z1 above means the next block to the south; replace it with the actual numerical Z coordinate.

Requirements:
- Both bed positions need valid floor support.
- Head and foot must be adjacent and form a valid bed.
- Confirm facing and part states.
- Keep access to the bed.
- Test sleeping and respawning in Survival mode.

B. DOUBLE CHEST

Chest block states include facing, type, and waterlogged. A double chest requires two adjacent compatible chest blocks with complementary left/right states.

Example candidate arrangement:

/setblock X 1 Z minecraft:chest[facing=north,type=left,waterlogged=false]
/setblock X1 1 Z minecraft:chest[facing=north,type=right,waterlogged=false]

X1 means the next block east; replace it with the actual numerical X coordinate.

IMPORTANT:
- Left/right assignment depends on facing and physical placement. Do not assume a universal west/east mapping.
- Safest method: place the first chest normally, place the second adjacent while facing the intended direction, then inspect the resulting block states.
- Both halves need valid support.
- Keep the space above the chest clear to guarantee lid access.
- Test that the chest forms one double inventory and opens correctly.

C. HANGING LANTERN

Example:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:lantern[hanging=true,waterlogged=false]

Attach it to a valid supporting block or suitable chain/support arrangement. Check headroom and route clearance.

D. STANDING LANTERN

Example:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:lantern[hanging=false,waterlogged=false]

Place it on a valid supporting surface. Verify the placement in-game.

E. TRAPDOOR SHUTTERS

Example state:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:oak_trapdoor[facing=north,half=bottom,open=false,powered=false,waterlogged=false]

Choose facing and half according to the actual wall face, hinge, and intended opening direction. Test both open and closed states. Do not apply one facing direction to every wall.

F. FLOWER POTS AND CANDLES

Examples:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:flower_pot
/setblock X Y Z minecraft:potted_poppy
/setblock X Y Z minecraft:candle[candles=1,lit=false,waterlogged=false]

A potted plant uses its own potted block ID. Pots and candles require valid support. Candle states may include candle count, lit, and waterlogged; use supported states for the chosen block.

G. HEARTH, CAMPFIRE, AND SMOKER

Correct campfire example:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:campfire[lit=true,signal_fire=false,waterlogged=false]

A campfire does not have a facing block state. Do not use facing=south.

Smoker example:

/setblock X Y Z minecraft:smoker[facing=south,lit=false]

Requirements:
- Campfire and smoker need valid placement/support.
- Keep functional space accessible.
- Keep fire away from flammable construction materials.
- Use stone or brick around the hearth.
- Build the chimney from suitable nonflammable materials.
- The chimney is decorative architecture; a chimney column is not required for campfire function.
- Test campfire and smoker operation in Survival mode.

============================================================
6. INTERIOR ZONING
============================================================

Divide the interior into four dedicated zones.

ZONE 1 — HEARTH AND COOKING
- Recessed stone or brick fireplace.
- Campfire.
- Smoker.
- Cauldron.
- Barrel pantry.
- Safe access to all functional blocks.
- Keep fire away from flammable materials and the main aisle.

ZONE 2 — CENTRAL LIVING ROOM
- Carpet runner.
- Seating and decorations.
- High timber rafter ceiling.
- Unobstructed central walking route, preferably 2 blocks wide where the footprint permits.
- No beams or decorations inside the required headroom envelope.

ZONE 3 — WORKBENCH AND CRAFTING
- Crafting table.
- Anvil.
- Double chest.
- Chiseled bookshelf.
- Clear interaction access to every workstation and storage block.
- Verify chest lid clearance.

ZONE 4 — STAIRCASE AND LOFT
- L-shaped oak staircase.
- Turning landing.
- Full stairwell cutout.
- Clear upper landing.
- No collision-bearing overhead decoration in the stair route.
- Adequate standing height beneath roof slopes.

FRONT ENTRANCE
- Flush threshold.
- Correctly installed two-block door.
- At least 2 blocks of standing clearance.
- Collision-free approach from porch to interior.

The 5 x 5 bay concept must be checked against the actual footprint. Do not assume it automatically provides enough space for a 2-block-wide aisle, furniture, staircase, and landing.

============================================================
8. COLLISION VALIDATION
============================================================

Inspect actual collision shapes. Do not use visual models alone.

Check these block categories:
- Full blocks.
- Bottom slabs.
- Top slabs.
- Stairs, including inner and outer shapes.
- Fences.
- Walls.
- Trapdoors in both states.
- Doors in both states.
- Lanterns.
- Carpets.
- Roof beams and decorative blocks.

Required test locations:
1. Front doorway.
2. Porch-to-interior transition.
3. Central living aisle.
4. Kitchen access.
5. Crafting area.
6. Every staircase step.
7. Staircase turning landing.
8. Loft entrance.
9. Loft walking area.
10. Roof-slope standing area.

JAVA DEBUGGING TOOLS
- F3 + B: show entity hitboxes.
- F3 + G: show chunk boundaries.
- Use the available Debug Settings collision-box visualization, if present in the client build.
- Walk the entire route in first person and third person.
- Test normal walking, sprinting, and crouching where relevant.

PASS CONDITION
No unintended collision, blocked movement, forced jumping or crouching, or interaction obstruction along the intended walking route.

============================================================
9. LIGHTING AND SURVIVAL COMPATIBILITY
============================================================

For the Overworld:
- Illuminate every accessible floor area.
- Inspect dark corners behind furniture.
- Check under and around stairs.
- Check the loft and roof-access areas.
- Do not rely on appearance alone to judge spawn safety.
- Verify the current spawn rules for the exact game version and dimension.

Creative mode is useful for construction and inspection. It does not replace Survival testing.

============================================================
10. CONSTRUCTION SEQUENCE
============================================================

Build in this order:

1. Foundation:
   Establish origin, dimensions, perimeter, and floor elevation.

2. Exterior walls:
   Construct walls, door opening, and window openings.

3. Doorway:
   Install door and verify threshold alignment.

4. Interior floor plan:
   Mark four zones and the central walking route.

5. Staircase:
   Build both flights, turning landing, and stairwell opening.

6. Loft:
   Construct upper floor around the stairwell opening.

7. Roof and beams:
   Construct roof while preserving required headroom.

8. Furniture:
   Place hearth, chests, bed, crafting stations, and decorations.

9. Lighting:
   Illuminate interior and exterior.

10. Testing:
    Complete collision, functionality, and Survival acceptance tests.
